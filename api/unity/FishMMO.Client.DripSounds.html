Remarks

Into water: the plink. What is heard of a drop falling into still water is barely its impact (a faint tick of a few milliseconds) and mostly the small bubble it pinches off a millisecond or two later: the bubble rings at its Minnaert frequency (about 3.3 kHz over its radius in millimetres, so 1–3 kHz for a drop's 1–3 mm bubble) and its pitch climbs as it rises and shrinks against the surface, dying within a tenth of a second. Not every drop traps one: some only plop, a duller knock and a low short ring of the water's surface.

On rock: the splat. A drop on wet rock is a short bright burst of a water film breaking (2–6 kHz, under a hundredth of a second) and its spatter landing round it a few tens of milliseconds after, softer.

The cave's ring is not in the clip: the drips play through the world's sound (WorldAudio's Effects), where the cave's reverb round the listener and the echoes off its far walls (AcousticEnvironment) give each its tail.
