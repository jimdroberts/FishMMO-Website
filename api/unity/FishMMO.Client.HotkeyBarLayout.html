Remarks

One flat index. The character's hotkey list, the wire and the database all address a slot by one number and know nothing of bars; a bar is SlotsPerBar consecutive slots (see HotkeySlotsPerBar for why that makes the width, unlike the count, unsafe to change once characters have bindings). Only the client's drawing and its key routing ever think in bars, and both go through here.

Keys are positions, bars are modifiers. The first bar's keys — left and right mouse, then 1 to 0 — name a POSITION on a bar. Which bar a press lands on is decided by the bar modifiers held at the moment of the press: the second bar's (Ctrl by default), the third's (Alt), and so on; with none held, the press lands on the first bar, or on the page a paged hotbar is resting on. So exactly one slot answers any press — there is no binding for "Ctrl+1" that could fire alongside the one for "1" — and rebinding a position key moves it on every bar at once. The rule is ResolvePressBar(int, bool, int) and its press/release pairing is HotkeyPressRouter; both are pure so they can be tested without an input device.

Parameters

heldModifierBar int

The first bar (in bar order, from the second) whose modifier is held, or -1 for none. Bar order is the tie-break when a player holds two: the lower bar wins, deterministically, rather than whichever modifier happened to be pressed last.

paged bool

True when the hotbar shows one bar at a time.

restingPage int

The bar a paged hotbar shows while no modifier is held.

Returns

int

The zero-based bar the press belongs to.

Remarks

A held modifier always wins, in either layout: Ctrl+1 is the second bar's first key whether or not that bar is the page on screen, so a player's hands do not have to know the layout. Without one, a stacked hotbar's unmodified keys are the first bar's — every bar is on screen and the first is the one they belong to — while a paged hotbar's are the resting page's, because that is the bar the player is looking at.
