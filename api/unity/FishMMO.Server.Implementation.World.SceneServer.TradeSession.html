Remarks

Pure state with the rules that govern it, and nothing else: no network, no containers, no Unity. TradeSystem resolves characters and inventories, validates what a request refers to, and then asks this object whether the change is allowed and records it here. That split is what lets the acceptance rules be tested as a truth table.

TWO STAGES: CONFIRM, THEN ACCEPT. Confirming says "this is my final offer". While both sides have confirmed the table is LOCKED: no item and no currency on either side can be changed at all, and only then may either party accept. That is what removes the last-minute switch as a category rather than as a race — there is no instant at which one player is reading a table the other can still edit. Changing your mind means revoking your confirmation first, which unlocks the table and clears both acceptances, in full view of the other player.

THE INVARIANT: every change to either offer clears both confirmations and both acceptances, and bumps Version. There is no way to alter what is on the table that leaves consent of either kind standing. TryConfirm(long, bool, uint) and TryAccept(long, bool, uint) both refuse a request that quotes any version but the current one, so a click that was in flight while the table changed lands as a no-op rather than as consent to something the player never saw.

Remarks

Stage two of two, and only reachable while IsLocked: there is nothing to accept until both sides have declared their offers final. The version quote is kept even though the table cannot change while locked, because an accept sent before a revoke-change-relock cycle would otherwise arrive as consent to a table the player never saw.

Withdrawing never fails on version: a player taking consent back must always be able to, whatever they were looking at. Only granting it is gated.

Remarks

Stage one of two. Once BOTH sides have confirmed the table is locked and nothing on it can change; that is what makes the accept that follows a decision about a settled table rather than a race against the other player's next click.

Revoking clears both acceptances. An acceptance is consent to a frozen table, and a revocation unfreezes it — so consent given on that basis cannot survive, on either side. The other party's CONFIRMATION is left standing: they have not changed their mind about their own offer, and making them re-click for someone else's revocation is noise. Any actual change to the table clears everything, as ever.

Revoking never fails on version, for the same reason withdrawing an acceptance does not: taking consent back must always be possible, whatever the player was looking at.

Remarks

Setting the same amount again is still a change: it bumps the version and clears acceptances. A client that re-sends its own value has told the server "I changed something", and the cost of honouring that literally is one extra round of accepts — far cheaper than a rule that lets some writes through without clearing consent.
