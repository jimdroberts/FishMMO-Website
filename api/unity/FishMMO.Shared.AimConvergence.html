Remarks

Why the aim is not simply the camera's forward. Shots start at the character's eye (CharacterAimOrigin, derived from the motor on every peer so a client cannot pick it), while the crosshair is on the camera's line, and the camera is never at the eye: in third person it sits behind and below it (the follow point is chest high), and over the shoulder it is also half a metre to the side. A shot along the camera's forward from the eye travels a line PARALLEL to the one the player aimed down, so it lands that offset away from the crosshair at every range: never on it.

The fix is to make the two lines meet. The camera's ray is traced into the world to find what the crosshair is on (the apex), and the aim is the direction from the eye to that point. The camera and the eye then form a V whose point is the thing the player is looking at, at whatever range it is.

Two guards keep the V sane. The camera's ray starts level with the eye, not at the camera, so nothing between the camera and the character (a wall behind them, their own back) can be the apex. And the apex is never closer than MinConvergenceDistance beyond that start: a crosshair resting on a post at the character's elbow would otherwise swing the aim to the side by up to ninety degrees.

The body is angled into the V too. The character faces, and walks toward, the same point, measured from where it is drawn (BodyHeading(Vector3, Vector3, Vector3)), so camera, character and shot all look at one point. The body's point is held at least BodyMinConvergenceDistance out, so the crosshair crossing from a near object to a far one turns the body by a few degrees rather than snapping it. The replicate carries the body's heading as a small yaw offset from the aim (HeadingOffset).

Computed by the owner only, in KCCPlayer.PopulateInput: the camera exists on no other peer. The result is quantised there before the owner predicts from it, like every other input (see AimDirectionCompression).

BodyMinConvergenceDistance

The nearest the point the BODY turns to (and walks toward) may be, beyond the character's level along the camera's ray.

public const float BodyMinConvergenceDistance = 6

Field Value

float

Remarks

Farther than the shot's minimum on purpose. The apex jumps whenever the crosshair crosses from something near to something far, and the body follows it with the facing's own snap; at 6 m the shoulder's half metre turns the body at most about five degrees, which reads as leaning into the aim rather than twitching. Shots are not held to this: they still converge exactly from MinConvergenceDistance.

MaxHeadingOffsetDegrees

The largest yaw the replicate may put between the aim and the body's heading: the whole circle.

public const float MaxHeadingOffsetDegrees = 180

Field Value

float

Remarks

The offset is measured from the heading of the AIM, and the aim's heading is not always near the body's. Over the shoulder looking down at nearby ground the eye is half a metre beside a ray that converges steeply, so the aim's heading swings toward the camera's side: 23 degrees off the body at 60 degrees down, 45 at 77, 79 at 89. Looking almost straight down while running, the eye leads the drawn body by more than the aim's few centimetres of ground run, and its heading points BACKWARDS: 180 degrees.

A bound of 45 degrees clamped those cases, and the body then faced (and walked) up to 135 degrees off the view. Bending the aim to fit instead moved the shot off the crosshair by 15 to 20 degrees. Neither is needed: the body's heading is the owner's to choose (it steers the character with its own input anyway), so nothing is gained by a modified client sending any value it likes. Two bytes on the wire for an offset over 16 degrees, sent only when it changes.

MaxOriginOffset

The farthest from the eye a launch line may start, metres.

public const float MaxOriginOffset = 1

Field Value

float

Remarks

The line starts where the third-person camera's ray passes the eye: the follow point's offset from the eye plus however far the drawn body trails the simulated one. Over the shoulder the shoulder's own displacement is taken out first (KCCPlayer.ResolveOwnerAim), so the projectile leaves the character, not the view's side. A metre covers every camera this game has, and is the whole of what a modified client can gain by lying: its projectile starts within a metre of where its eye is, and the path from the eye to the line is still checked for cover (AbilityObject.ResolveSpawnPose).

Remarks

The floor was a distance along the ray. Looking down, that distance covers less and less ground (by the cosine of the pitch) while the camera's sideways offset stays what it is, so with the crosshair on the ground nearby the body turned 10 degrees off the view at 60 down, 19 at 75 and 45 at 85, and walked that way. BodyHeading(Vector3, Vector3, Vector3)

The heading the body turns to: from where the character is DRAWN to its BodyPoint(Vector3, Vector3, Vector3, float, Vector3), flat.

public static Vector3 BodyHeading(Vector3 drawnPosition, Vector3 bodyPoint, Vector3 cameraForward)

Parameters

drawnPosition Vector3
bodyPoint Vector3
cameraForward Vector3

Returns

Vector3

Remarks

From the drawn character, not the eye, because the view is built around the drawn one: the camera follows the smoothed body, which trails the simulated motor (and so the eye) by the owner's interpolation. Measured from the eye, a strafing character would be off the crosshair's line by that lag, turn toward it, and so steer its own strafe into a slow circle. Measured from the drawn body, the answer depends only on the camera's layout:

  • third and first person: the drawn character is on the crosshair's line, so this is the camera's heading exactly, as before;
  • over the shoulder: the character is the shoulder's offset left of that line, so this turns it in toward the crosshair, by about five degrees at the nearest and almost nothing at range.

Remarks

With a launch line given, from the point beside the eye it names to the point its distance along the aim: the camera's ray through the crosshair, rebuilt. Without one, from the eye along the aim.

A pure function of the replicated input and the eye every peer derives, so the owner and the server simulate the same projectile to the last bit, and no trace is involved. The offset is held to MaxOriginOffset whatever was sent.
