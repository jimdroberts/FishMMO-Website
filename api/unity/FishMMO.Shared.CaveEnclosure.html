Where a carved cave's air is, as the lit surfaces' underground darkness reads it (2026-10-21): per column of the world's Cell grid under the cave, the lowest and highest of the rock's inner faces (the floor and the roof seen from inside) and how shut in from the daylight the column is, 0 at a mouth or under a skylight to 1 deep in. Baked by the cut from the cave's own rock, kept on its CaveVisibility.

Remarks

Why. How far under the ground a surface lies (FishGroundMap.hlsl FishUnderground, from the 5 m ground map) was all that kept the sky and the sun off a cave's rock: no sky from twenty metres in, all of it within six. A hall under a hillside that falls away has only a few metres of rock over its far end, which then took the daylight, and past the shadow distance the sun too — far walls and floors lit grey that went dark as they were walked up to (Moron 2's lava tube). Inside a cave the air is the cave's however thin the rock over it: a point between a column's floor and roof is underground by the column's closure.

Never the hillside. The cut keeps its outer shell CaveSystem.CutSlab under the ground and its roof a shell's thickness under that, so the ground over a column, and all that stands on it, is metres above the column's roof: past the gate's Margin. Only at a mouth may the rock come up to the ground, and there the closure is 0.

One reading. At(Vector3) mirrors FishGroundMap.hlsl's FishCaveEnclosed line for line (the window CaveEnclosureMap gathers the live caves into): the four columns round a point filtered by their closure, those that hold cave rock only, and the point gated by the most open of their floor-to-roof spans. Change both together.
