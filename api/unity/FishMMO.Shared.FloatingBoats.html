Remarks

Why not props. The POI props are static and instanced; the sea is not (SeaTide moves it up to a few metres), so a hull fixed at mean sea level would stand clear of the water at low tide and be swamped at high.

A moored boat collides (2026-10-12: "Add collision to floating boats so we don't swim through them"). Its root, which the server keeps, carries its hull's collider and moves only with the tide: its level is worked out from WaterQuery at the synced tick, on the server and every client alike and to the millimetre, so a prediction meets the hull where the server does (and the tide moves it a tenth of a millimetre a second). Its bob and roll on the swell are its client-only visual child's alone.

A ship under way collides too, and its pose is a pure function of the synced tick. Each ship's root (kept by the server, its hull's collider on it, no NetworkObject) is placed on its lane by CourseAt(Lane, in Sailing, double) from tick × TickDelta and on the water by WaterQuery at the same tick. Nothing about it is ever sent: the server and every client already agree on the tick, so they agree on the hull. That is the whole reason not to make it a KCCPlatform: a platform is STEPPED (its next pose depends on its last), so FishNet has to reconcile it to every observer every tick and roll it back under a replaying rider, which costs a forwarded NetworkObject, a reconcile per observer per tick, and an observer range — and a ship is seen from kilometres off, sails for an hour, and twelve of them share a scene. A pose that is a function of the tick needs none of it: to "roll the hull back" for a replayed tick is simply to evaluate the function at that tick.

When it is posed. On every peer, before anything simulates a tick: in TimeManager.OnPreTick for the live tick, in PredictionManager.OnPreReplicateReplay for each tick a client replays (so a replayed ground probe meets the deck where it stood on that tick, as a forwarded platform's would), and again in OnPostReconcile to stand it back at the live tick before OnTick runs; then Physics.SyncTransforms, because the project runs with automatic transform sync off and a moved collider is otherwise invisible to queries until the next simulation. The tick it is posed at is the SYNCED tick the server runs that input on — the server's own Tick, a client's LocalTick mapped through the last reconcile's pairing (WeatherExposureTick) — which is the same tick a rider's replicate reads (KCCController.WaterTick), live and in replay.

Riders are carried through the same seam as a platform's (KinematicCharacterMotor.SetPlatformVelocity, the attached-rigidbody carry): TryGetCarry(Scene, uint, double, Vector3, float, out Vector3, out float, out ShipRef) gives the velocity that keeps a deck point under the rider's feet from the last tick's pose to this one's — a rigid motion, so the 180° swing at anchor carries a rider round with the deck — and the yaw the hull turned through between the two, which the motor puts on the rider's facing as KCC does an attached PhysicsMover's rotation; both only to a stably grounded character. Who is aboard is decided spatially, again from the tick (feet over the deck in the last tick's frame, confirmed by a ray down onto this hull's own collider), never from a trigger volume or the motor's ground collider, neither of which a reconcile restores.

A rider's camera turns with the ship (the owner's only, a presentation of the drawn hull: see TryGetDrawnYaw(in ShipRef, out float)), as KCC's camera turns with a PhysicsMover. It has to: a character facing the camera (the shipped orientation) takes its facing from the camera's rotation in its input every tick, so a camera fixed in the world would hold the rider's facing fixed in the world whatever the motor did. Being input, the turned camera reaches the server and every replay as it is, so nothing about it can diverge.

Drawn on the client from the same function at a fractional tick, VisualInterpolationTicks behind the simulated one: the lag the owner's own character is drawn at (its NetworkObject smoother), so a rider and the deck under them are drawn at the same moment and do not slide against each other. Bob, roll and the wind's heel are the drawn hull's alone; the collider never bobs.

A ship beyond the scene's edge (waiting at a lane end that is open sea) is hidden and its collider and obstacle are switched off: nothing can run into a ship nobody can see.

Airships (a cloud world's, 2026-10-14: "flying ships") are the same ships on lanes through the air (Airborne): a lane point's y is the ship's own height, climbing and descending between landing stages, and the root's frame has the deck at y 0 instead of the waterline. At a port end such a lane holds the ship still alongside its stage and swings it only at the end of its wait (BerthSwing). Everything else (the pose from the tick, the carry, the solid hull) is the ships' own.

Built by the cut: a harbour's (PointOfInterestHarbour), the sea lanes' (PointOfInterestSeaTraffic) and the sky's (PointOfInterestSkyTraffic), each on an object the server keeps, each boat's drawn hull under a ClientOnlyObject.
