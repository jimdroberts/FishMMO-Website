A site's footprint seen from above (2026-10-18: "Towns, cities and all other points of interest need different carving besides just circles"): how far its ground reaches from its anchor toward each of a ring of evenly spaced bearings, the first due north (+z), turning east (+x) as a yaw does. Between two bearings its edge runs straight, so the footprint is a star-shaped polygon: every point of it is seen from the anchor, and a ray from the anchor leaves it exactly once.

Remarks

One footprint, read by everything. The pad's levelling, the keep-outs, the ways' owner marks and carve, the layout's boundary, the walls, the moat, the fields round a town and its region all read the same outline. The site's radius stays its bounding reach (no point of the outline is farther), so spacing and culling by radius still hold.

An empty outline is a circle of the radius it is read with: every site planned before outlines (and every kind that keeps a disc, a bridge or a mill on its bank) reads exactly as it did.
