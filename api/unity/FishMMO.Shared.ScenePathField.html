Remarks

Why a distance field. The splat is two metres a texel: a trail under a metre wide painted there is a smear four metres across. A distance to the nearest path's EDGE, bilinearly filtered, is exact along a straight stretch at any magnification, so the shader can cut a crisp edge, fray it with noise, lay two wheel ruts a gauge apart and leave grass between them — none of which a weight could say.

Why pages. Paths cover a few percent of a scene. A whole-scene field at this resolution would be hundreds of megabytes; the pages a path touches are a few thousand, packed into one atlas. A page table (one texel a page) says where each page is in the atlas; a page holds 32 × 32 texels plus a one-texel border copied from its neighbours' ground, so a bilinear fetch never reads another page's texels.

A texel (RGBA8, linear): r the signed distance to the nearest path's edge (−8 m inside … +3 m outside), g that path's half width (÷ 8 m), b its wear, a its surface code (SurfaceCode(ScenePathSurface)).

Where two paths meet the edge is the nearer of theirs, and the rest is a blend of every way whose edge lies within BlendMetres of it, the nearer weighing more: a trail's earth runs on into the road it joins and thins into the gravel, rather than stopping at the line where the two edges are equally far (2026-10-19: "hard texture cuts" — the nearest way alone took the texel, so surface, wear and width all switched across one texel). Except inside a building's floor (Floor), whose wear and surface nothing else drawn over it blends into (a door path's round end or a well's apron reaching through the wall would bring its lower wear, and so its grass, indoors); its edge is still the deepest of them, so the floor is as bare as the apron round it.

Pure data, built by the cut and baked to two textures beside the terrain (ScenePathBake), which ScenePathSurfaceBinder binds.
