An opening in a cave's roof up to the sky (2026-10-20, caves livened; Skylight): where its emitter stands and how its seed carries the opening's height over it, and the two measures every presenter of it agrees on — how much of the sky a point under it sees (SkyShare(Vector3, float, Vector3)) and whether the sun falls on it through the opening (InBeam(Vector3, float, Vector3, Vector3)). One definition read by the client's skylight presenter (CaveSkylights: the shaft, the motes, the sky let in round it), its bounce light (SiteLightPool) and the lit surfaces' underground darkness (FishSkylights.hlsl, which mirrors these two functions line for line).

Remarks

Why the sky's share. Underground the lit surfaces take no sky and no sun (FishUnderground: their ambient and the sun's term are taken off, since the cave's deep rock casts no shadow). Under an opening that is wrong: the throat's walls near the top are as good as outdoors, and the floor under it sees a patch of sky — the share a disc of the opening's radius fills of a point's sky (r² / (r² + h²) on its axis), boosted a little for the light the throat's sunlit walls throw down, falling off faster off the axis where the rock hides the opening.

Why the beam. The sun's term is kept, not taken off, where a line toward the sun leaves through the opening: the shadow map (the terrain's, its hole clipped; the rock round the opening, which casts) then lights exactly the patch the sun reaches and no more.
