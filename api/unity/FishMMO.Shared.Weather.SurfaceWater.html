Remarks

A sea has nothing to hit. The weather finds the highest surface around the camera by casting rays down, and a ray goes straight through water to the sea bed. So rain and snow fell through the sea and landed on the sand under it, splashes burst on the bottom, and the lens kept its raindrops with the camera three metres down. Wherever the ground is lower than the water, the water is the highest surface.

Published by the water, read by the weather. The water depends on the game and never the other way round, so the weather cannot ask it — each body of water registers itself here, the way the server registers its weather host into WeatherTimelines.Commands.

One sea, any number of inland waters. The sea stands at one level everywhere its ground is lower (TryGetLevel(out float)). A lake or a river stands only where it is (SurfaceWater.IAreaSource), so the question that covers both is the water at a place: TryGetSurfaceAt(float, float, out float).

Remarks

Presentation only. The movement floats on the still surface (WaterQuery, a synced tick and no waves, so the server and every client agree); a figure drawn on that line sits still while the sea heaves through it. Added to the still line, this is the drawn sea at the point: the figure rides the waves the player sees. It reads the frame's camera and clock, so it is never for anything simulated.
