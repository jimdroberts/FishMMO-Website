What one term of an exposure state reads: a channel of the blended weather, or one of the driver's physical air properties.

public enum WeatherExposureInput : byte

Fields

AirHumidity = 2

0 dry air … 1 saturated. Present without any rain: this is what muggy is.

AirInstability = 3

0 … 1: how willing this air is to build a storm, before it has built one.

AirLowPressure = 1

0 a settled high … 1 the middle of a deep low.

AirPolarNight = 6

0 … 1: how far the sun has stopped rising here. A polar winter, by itself.

AirTower = 4

0 … 1: how strongly a storm tower stands over this spot.

AirWindSpeed = 5

0 calm … 1 at 30 m/s. The driver's real wind, not the channel's blended one.

Channel = 0

A channel of the blended weather. The default, and what most states want.

Remarks

The channels are the weather as it looks — how much is falling, how hard the wind blows, how thick the fog is. The air properties are the physics underneath it: the pressure field, how much water the air can hold, how willing it is to build a storm. They are not the same thing, and some states are only expressible in the second. "Soaked" is rain, a channel. "Clammy" is saturated air, which is present with no rain at all. "Ears popping" is a deep low, which is invisible in every channel there is.

All of them are normalised to 0..1 so Onset and Full mean the same thing whichever is chosen — including AirLowPressure, which is the driver's signed pressure read as depth-of-low so that, like every other input here, more of it drives harder.
